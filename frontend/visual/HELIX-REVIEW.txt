HELIX-ONLY REVIEW

Criterion: does it look like a real molecule, or like a chart?
Final judgment: it reads as a molecule. The continuous round backbones, enclosed
volume, softly rounded rungs, depth-dependent scale and light, and correct
occlusion establish a physical object. The model turns around its fixed long
axis; there is no translating layer or scrolling waveform.

Scope
Only the decorative helix, its mounting/cleanup, and its review tooling changed.
Page wording, values, report rendering, questions, question/finish announcements,
notification settings, typography and palette remain as accepted. The phone helix
moves above the headline as requested. The running card and the downstream page
retain their previous dimensions (desktop 1440x1126; phone 390x1751).

Round 0 — first projected molecule
Captures: /root/reviews/ui-shots/helix/round-0
Replaced the scrolling SVG waves/dots with projected 3D tube faces and rungs.
This immediately read as a molecule, but face seams and longitudinal stripes made
the surface resemble braided rope. The rung joints looked clipped and the tube
ends looked square-cut. The desktop size and above-headline phone placement were
calm and legible.

Round 1 — smooth material and genuine depth buffering
Captures: /root/reviews/ui-shots/helix/round-1
Moved the principal renderer to self-hosted three.js/WebGL. Smooth tube normals
replace surface seams; rounded caps finish the backbones and capsule-shaped rungs
meet them cleanly. A perspective camera makes nearer surfaces thicker. Fixed
lighting and forest-colored depth fog make the far surfaces dimmer. The depth
buffer resolves crossings per pixel. Three muted rung tones stay within the
existing palette. Independent review agreed this reads as a molecule. The phone
copy needed a little more breathing room above the time divider.

Round 2 — quiet finish and long-session behavior
Final captures: /root/reviews/ui-shots/helix/running-{desktop,phone}.png
Refined near/far separation without adding glossy highlights. Reduced the phone
illustration block by 12px and transferred that room below the copy, preserving
card height and the positions of timing and controls. Batched repeated rungs and
end caps into instances, reducing draw calls from 30 to seven. The renderer uses
no shadows, post-processing, textures, external requests or retained drawing-
buffer copies. Final visual review found no chart cues, clipping or competition
with the headline. No further visual changes were needed.

Rendering and lifecycle
- three.js 0.180.0 is pinned, licensed and bundled into the existing demo.js.
- The model rotates around its local x axis every 10 seconds, or 14 seconds while
  waiting for an answer. Its position, camera and slight diagonal tilt stay fixed.
- Drawing is capped below 30fps by a 34ms minimum timer scheduled after each draw.
  Phase follows elapsed time, so slow frames do not slow the rotation period.
- Hidden tabs cancel the drawing timer. Resuming retains the phase rather than
  jumping through time spent hidden.
- Reduced motion paints one still frame and has no animation timer. A resize
  redraws the same pose to fit the new dimensions.
- The same canvas survives status polling and question arrival. Terminal runs
  dispose geometry, materials, instance buffers and the WebGL context.
- WebGL context loss pauses drawing; restoration resumes it. When WebGL is
  unavailable, a perspective-projected, depth-sorted 2D tube renderer is available.
- Render resolution is capped at 1.5x device pixel ratio. The illustration is
  decorative and hidden from assistive technology; it depicts no run data.

Validation
- npm test: 133 frontend tests passed, including ten geometry/lifecycle tests.
- Existing Python demo suite: 89 tests and 41 subtests passed.
- Full workspace browser checks: 245 passed across all 18 views and both
  notification-permission scenarios. Zero axe violations; all 14,790 audited
  text glyphs use the existing self-hosted fonts.
- Native-timer helix browser checks: 36 passed across desktop and DPR2 phone.
  Rotation rate, <=30fps cap, fixed canvas, live reduced motion, hidden-tab pause,
  polling continuity, terminal disposal and the existing CSP all pass.
- The browser uses ANGLE/SwiftShader software WebGL in this container. Its short
  samples ran about 23–24fps with about 0.064–0.067s JavaScript per 2.12s and zero
  layout work. Total task time was about 1.72–1.73s in this software-rendered
  environment. These measurements are not a claim about hardware GPU CPU use or battery life.

Both running videos are exactly 14 seconds long and start after the self-hosted
fonts load. Their manifests confirm WebGL, a 10000ms rotation period, moving
frames, no external requests and no browser errors. Observed drawing during
capture stayed below 30fps (desktop 20.73; phone 27.85 in this software-rendered
environment). First-frame posters were reviewed after encoding.
